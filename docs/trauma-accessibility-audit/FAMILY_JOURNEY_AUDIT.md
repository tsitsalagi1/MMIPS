## Trauma-informed principle evaluation

- Safety: Strong intent in homepage, submission notice, photo privacy, no-rumor language, and map approximation. Blocked by lack of review-before-submit, reference number, robust urgent hide, and data-preserving CAPTCHA/error recovery.
- Trustworthiness: Good repeated statement that MMIPS is not law enforcement and does not publish automatically. Risk where wording says MMIPS "checks" or "verified" before operational proof and where alerts UI appears actionable but is not.
- Transparency: Partial. Public/private distinctions exist in copy but are not consistently embedded beside every sensitive field.
- Collaboration: Partial. Correction/removal route exists, but no family-facing request-more-information or public preview process is evident.
- Family voice and choice: Partial. Photo permissions are a strength. Missing save/return, review-before-submit, and clear withdrawal path reduce choice.
- Empowerment: Partial. Users can submit/correct, but repeated entry and unclear failure states may disempower.
- Cultural respect: Intent is respectful, but source review cannot verify Indigenous reviewer input, culturally specific resource quality, or language preferences.
- Resistance to retraumatization: Partial. Plain calm language is present. Risks remain around summary fields, identifying-detail captions, urgent defaults, and failure recovery.

## Proposed release gate changes for later reconciliation

Do not edit shared release gates in this task. Later gate updates should require: review-before-submit with public/private separation; reference number; data-preserving error recovery or explicit reattachment guidance for uploads; accessible CAPTCHA alternative; urgent hide operational SLA; alerts unavailable/working honesty; map/list parity; independent human review closure.
