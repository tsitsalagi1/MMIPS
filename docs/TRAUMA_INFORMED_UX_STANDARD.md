# MMIPS Trauma-Informed UX and Accessibility Standard

MMIPS serves families and communities who may be frightened, grieving, exhausted, or under severe cognitive and emotional strain. Design must reduce harm, avoid blame, preserve choice, and keep private information private.

## Principles

- Use calm, direct, respectful language.
- Explain why information is requested and whether it may become public.
- Separate public candidate information from private reviewer-only information.
- Preserve user control: review, edit, pause, and correction/removal paths must be clear.
- Avoid urgency pressure except for true emergency guidance.
- Do not imply responsibility, blame, or disbelief.
- Never ask for graphic details unless a legally reviewed workflow explicitly requires private reviewer-only handling.

## Family-facing forms

- Keep sections short with clear headings.
- Use examples that are synthetic and labeled as examples.
- Identify required fields clearly.
- Provide plain-language errors and recovery paths.
- Confirm that MMIPS is not law enforcement and does not replace emergency or official reporting.
- Make private-by-default behavior explicit for submissions, requester information, photos, exact locations, and moderator notes.

## Public pages

- Use neutral, factual, verified wording.
- Route tips to official/family-approved contacts.
- Do not publish rumors, suspect accusations, exact sensitive locations, or private family details.
- Include easy correction/removal access.

## Accessibility target

WCAG 2.2 AA is the target. Every family-facing and public-facing workflow must support:

- Keyboard operation.
- Visible focus states.
- Programmatic labels and instructions.
- Meaningful headings and landmarks.
- AA color contrast.
- Error identification and suggestions.
- Reduced cognitive load and no time pressure without extension.
- Accessible alternatives to maps and visual flyers.

## Review requirement

Before real submissions are enabled, a human reviewer with family-support, victim-services, Indigenous community, legal, or comparable expertise must review the experience and findings must be tracked.
