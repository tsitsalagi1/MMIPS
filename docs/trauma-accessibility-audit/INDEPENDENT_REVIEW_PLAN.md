# MMIPS Independent Review Plan

No independent review has occurred as part of this audit.

## Purpose

Before real submissions are enabled, MMIPS should invite qualified reviewers to evaluate trauma-informed family experience, cultural respect, privacy clarity, and accessibility using synthetic scenarios only. Consultation is evidence for improvement, not blanket approval.

## Reviewer experience sought

- MMIP family-support or Indigenous victim-services experience.
- Trauma-informed practice experience.
- Disability/accessibility testing experience.
- Lived experience only when voluntary, supported, compensated where possible, and never required.

## Safeguards

- Use synthetic/demo cases only; never ask a reviewer to disclose personal trauma.
- Explain confidentiality, what will be recorded, who can see notes, and how findings are tracked.
- Allow reviewers to pause, skip, or stop without explanation.
- Compensate reviewers where possible and respect time boundaries.
- Do not treat one reviewer as representative of all Indigenous peoples, all Tribes, all affected families, or all disabled users.
- Distinguish consultation, legal/policy review, accessibility testing, and final launch approval.

## Process

1. Prepare synthetic staging environment with no production credentials and no real personal data.
2. Provide a plain-language orientation: what MMIPS is, what it is not, and what feedback is requested.
3. Ask reviewers to walk through homepage, submit, confirmation, corrections/urgent hide, public profile/flyer/share, resources/policies, alerts, map/list, and selected admin preview flows if appropriate.
4. Offer supported note-taking by a facilitator trained not to probe for trauma narratives.
5. Record findings as severity, affected flow, evidence, recommendation, dependency, and whether reviewer wants follow-up.
6. Triage findings with product/security/legal/operations owners.
7. Block launch on unresolved critical trauma-informed, privacy, cultural-respect, or accessibility concerns.
8. Share a respectful summary back to reviewers when appropriate, without implying endorsement.

## Severity

- Critical: likely safety/privacy harm, retraumatization, inaccessible core task, false official/operational promise, or inability to correct/hide unsafe public information.
- High: substantial burden, confusion, missing choice, or likely WCAG AA barrier in important tasks.
- Medium: clarity, consistency, or cognitive-load issue that should be fixed before broad use.
- Low: polish or minor wording issue.
- Informational: observation or future enhancement.

## Tracking requirements

Each review finding must link to a backlog ID, owner, target workstream, resolution status, evidence, and retest result. Unresolved critical findings block real submissions and public launch.
